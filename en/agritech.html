    Agritech · Our priority sector

    When technological innovation helps African agriculture grow.

    Mapping, satellite imagery, traceability, mobile money payments: we design, build and deploy digital tools for agricultural value chains, for the producers, cooperatives, exporters and buyers who source in Africa.

    Test my EUDR readiness Our areas of work
    What technology changes

    How technological innovation improves our agriculture.

    Eight technologies we put to work for agricultural value chains, and what they can deliver in practice.

    Offline mobile apps

    Field agents enter data at source, on the plantation, without waiting for a network.

    Impact: no more lost paper forms or re-entry.

    GPS and GIS

    Each plot is delineated, measured and linked to its producer on a map.

    Impact: actual plot areas and a well-managed supply base.

    Satellite imagery and drones

    Monitoring of forest cover, crop condition and land-use change.

    Impact: a way to check remotely for deforestation, in support of your due diligence.

    Artificial intelligence

    Risk analysis, yield estimation, detection of anomalies in the data.

    Impact: signals to help prepare the season.

    Sensors and connected devices

    Local weather, soil moisture and storage conditions measured continuously.

    Impact: fewer post-harvest losses, better quality.

    Digital traceability

    QR codes, secure registries and volume reconciliation, from the producer to the port.

    Impact: every bag linked to its origin.

    Mobile money and fintech

    Traceable payments, premiums paid without cash, histories that can be used for credit.

    Impact: traceable payments, with no need to transport cash.

    Digital agricultural advice

    Advice, prices and alerts sent to producers by SMS or WhatsApp.

    Impact: the right practices at the right time.

    Areas of work

    Seven areas of work, from plot to report.

    Each area can be a project in its own right or part of a complete system, designed for your organisation.

    Plot mapping and polygon mapping

    Field agents survey the boundaries of each plantation by GPS, in a data collection app that works offline. Areas are calculated automatically and each plot is linked to its producer.

    • Offline polygon capture with accuracy checks
    • Area calculation and overlap detection
    • GeoJSON, KML and Shapefile exports
    • Annual updates and change history
    • Migration and cleaning of existing maps
    For whom

    Exporters, cooperatives, sustainability programmes, reforestation projects.

    Key outcome

    A reliable map of your entire supply base.

    Discuss it with an expert

    Supply chain traceability

    From the producer to the cooperative, then to the warehouse and the port, every transaction is recorded. A QR code per bag or per lot makes it possible to trace back to the origin.

    • Farm-gate and buying-station purchases, online or offline
    • QR code labels per bag, lot and truck
    • Reconciliation of volumes purchased, stored and shipped, with alerts on inconsistencies
    • Portal for sharing data with your buyers
    For whom

    Exporters, intermediary buyers, cooperatives and unions.

    Key outcome

    Every bag linked to its producers and plots.

    Discuss it with an expert

    EUDR compliance and certifications

    We turn your field data into a complete and consistent compliance file: deforestation risk analysis, volume consistency, audit-ready documents.

    • Analysis of plots against forest cover maps
    • Risk assessment and mitigation, plot by plot
    • Data prepared for your buyers’ due diligence statements
    • Data ready for certification audits

    EUDR compliance

    Your European buyers are asking for evidence. We help you gather it.

    The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) requires cocoa, coffee, rubber, oil palm, soya, wood and cattle placed on the EU market, or exported from the EU, to be “deforestation-free”, produced in accordance with the relevant legislation of the country of production and covered by a due diligence statement.

    —days

    Until the regulation applies to large and medium-sized operators and traders, on 30 December 2026. Micro and small operators: 30 June 2027 (30 December 2026 for those already covered by the EU Timber Regulation).

    • 01
      Geolocation of every plotA GPS point for a plot of 4 hectares or less, a full polygon above that.
    • 02
      No deforestation after 31 December 2020Cross-checked against satellite imagery.
    • 03
      Lot-by-lot traceabilityEvery bag linked to its producers and plots.
    • 04
      Due diligence statementThe data and evidence your buyers need for their statement.
